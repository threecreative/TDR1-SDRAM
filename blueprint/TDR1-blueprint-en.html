TDR1 SDRAM

REV 0.1.0
2026-10-16
STATUS: CONCEPT

Main memory that moves three data transfers per clock period. One external clock, three internal phases at 0°, 120° and 240°, and ECC built into every burst. Laid out as a JEDEC-style module that can sit alongside DDR5.

SHEET 1Module layout

Top view of a single-rank TDR1 UDIMM with x8 chips. The outline matches a 288-pin DDR5 UDIMM, but the key notch sits elsewhere, so the module cannot go into a DDR5 slot. The clock driver (TCKD) generates the three phases on the module itself.

SHEET 2Timing and burst

Each clock period carries three bits per data pin, one on each phase. A burst has 18 transfers: 16 data and 2 ECC. That makes a burst exactly 6 clock periods long, and a 64-byte cache line fits it cleanly.

SHEET 3Chip architecture

One 36 Gb TDR1 chip: 32 Gb for data and 4 Gb for check bits. The memory core resembles DDR5, with 8 bank groups of 4 banks. New are the 3-phase DLL, the 18:1 serializer per data pin, and the ECC/CRC check on writes.

NOTELimits of this design

  • Per pin, TDR1 is no easier than DDR5: at 9600 MT/s a bit lasts 104 ps, just like DDR5-9600. The gain is in the clock and the power, not in the signal itself.
  • The three phases must match to within about 2% of a bit time. Any mismatch costs margin and is trained out at boot.
  • A new memory controller is required. A standard DDR5 processor cannot drive TDR1.
  • A real TDR1 chip needs a DRAM fab. The first feasible prototype is a simulation and an FPGA model, as laid out in the specification.
